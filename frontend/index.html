About the assessment platform

A plain-language explanation of how the platform turns uploaded documents into structured, evidence-based assessments.

A guided interoperability assessment methodology

This platform supports interoperability assessment of legal instruments, policy files, administrative process designs and digital public-service specifications. It follows a staged assessment methodology rather than treating the document as a plain text summary task. The tool reads the source, identifies binding requirements, checks whether those requirements may affect digital public services, maps the affected services and actors, analyses interoperability effects and then turns the findings into risks, recommendations and validation checks.

1. Source-grounded readingThe uploaded document is treated as the evidence base. The assessment keeps track of relevant excerpts so that findings can be traced back to the source rather than presented as unsupported conclusions.
2. Binding requirementsThe tool looks for obligations, conditions, procedural duties and governance rules that may affect digital public services, information systems, registries, data exchanges or cross-border workflows.
3. Framework-guided assessmentThe Interoperable Europe Act is used for trigger logic, the assessment guidelines guide the reporting method, EIF supports layer-based interoperability analysis and EIRA can add an architecture and reuse perspective when selected.
4. Contextual signal layerThe sensitive-language lexicon is not a blacklist. Terms are interpreted in context to identify legacy administrative patterns, under-specified digital procedures or possible interoperability risks.
5. Traceable recommendationsRecommendations are connected to specific risks, binding requirements and implementation dependencies, so the user can see why a proposed change is needed.
6. Validation before final reviewA validation layer challenges unsupported, overconfident or insufficiently evidenced conclusions. The result is a decision-support assessment intended for human review, not an automatic institutional decision.
